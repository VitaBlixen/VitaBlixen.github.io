They needed a simple informational site aimed at business partners who
wanted to read about the company. Two things shaped everything else. Nobody
in the company writes code, and they would be the ones keeping the site
current long after I was done. And as a young company, they had very little
to spend.

## The decision not to code it

I could have built the site by hand. It would have looked the way I wanted,
and it would have been the better piece of work to show off.

It would also have left them with a site they could not change. Every
update to a price, a description, or a new partner would have to go through
me, and a company with no budget cannot keep paying someone to change a
paragraph. The right solution was the one that still worked on the day I
stopped being involved.

I looked at WordPress and Wix first. I ended up building it in the site
builder at Simply.com, because it was cheap, it was already where their
domain and mail lived, and it was simple enough that they could handle it
themselves after handover.

## The video backgrounds

They wanted looping video in the background of two or three of the
subpages. The site builder had no direct way to upload video for that.

Rather than abandon it or move the whole project to something more
complicated, I found a way through: the builder could take a video as a
section background if it came from YouTube, so the videos went up as
unlisted uploads on an account belonging to the company. Unlisted meant
they were not public on YouTube, but the site could still play them. I
tested it on the live site before committing to it.

The founder sent me video and photo material along with a PDF of the
content he wanted on the site. From there the build was mine to work out
on my own.

## Learning the tools I needed

The coding was not the demanding part of this project. What stretched me
were the things I had not done before. I wanted the site to feel alive
rather than flat, and that meant editing the video for the backgrounds and
reworking photos so they held up across a full-width section.

I had used Photoshop a little during my studies. I had never edited video
at all. I learned enough Premiere to cut and loop what I needed, and
enough Photoshop for the images, by working out what specifically I needed
to know rather than learning the programs properly.

Neither program is central to the work I am looking for. What it does say
is that I can pick up an unfamiliar tool quickly and get the job finished
with it.

## Handover

The point of the whole approach was that they could take over. I set up the
accounts the site needed in their own name, so nothing depended on me, and
handed the lot over with the passwords for them to change.

## What I took from it

The solution I would have enjoyed building most was not the solution the
client needed. Choosing for the person who has to maintain something after
you leave is a real design decision, and it is one I would make the same
way again.
