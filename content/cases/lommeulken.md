## The problem

Lommeulken is a student angling association. The members arrange fishing
trips together and run a competition through the year for the largest
catch. Their website was built in the 2000s and had not been changed
since.

That showed in two ways. It looked dated, and the association wanted
something that felt current. More importantly, it was built before
smartphones, and the phone was where the members actually used it. They
read posts and news there, and above all they registered their catches
there, standing by the water on a trip rather than at a desk afterwards.

The site also had visitors who were not members, other anglers who
followed the catches and the historical records from earlier seasons.
Together with the association we set that aside and agreed on three
things to focus on: registering a catch, signing up new members, and
creating a fishing trip that catches could be registered under.

## My role

We were four students. Two of them built the site in React, and one led
the report and the academic side of the project. I ran the workshop, built
the prototypes in Figma, and did the user testing. We worked in SCRUM with
sprints and running check-ins.

## What I did

I ran a workshop with the association, inspired by the Future Workshop
method. It was physical, and it gave us the problems above along with a
priority order for what the prototype needed to cover.

From there I built interactive prototypes in Figma, both of the core
functions and of how the site could be structured. I tested them with
three representatives from the association. They opened the prototype
from a link and worked through it while we talked. I made adjustments and
tested a second time, and then the two developers built from the result.

## What the testing changed

My first prototype did not include a way to create a fishing trip. The
first round of testing made clear that the members wanted it, and not only
to register their own catches. They wanted to open a specific trip and see
which fish had been caught on it. It went into the second version, and
into the site.

## Decisions

**Mobile first, not mobile as well.** The competition depended on members
registering a catch while they were still out. A design that worked on a
desktop and shrank down would have missed the moment the site was actually
used.

**A narrow scope, agreed early.** The public part of the site had real
traffic, and it was tempting to redesign everything. We chose the three
member journeys instead, because those were the ones the association ran
on. A smaller scope meant we could test it properly within the course.

**Prototype before code.** Two rounds of testing in Figma cost a few days
and changed what got built. The developers started from something the
members had already been through twice.

## Outcome

The prototypes and the structure were handed over to the two developers,
who built the site in React. One of them sat on the board of the
association and carried the work on after the course ended. I do not know
what became of the site after that.

## What I would do differently

We only ever spoke to the four contacts we had in the association. The
workshop and both rounds of testing were with the same small group, and
with that few people a Future Workshop is a loose affair. I had run the
same method with a proper number of participants in my bachelor project
the year before, so I knew during the project what we were missing. I
would put more effort into reaching members beyond the immediate contacts,
and I would raise it earlier instead of working around it.

I would also test somewhere closer to where the site is used. We tested
online, over a link, sitting still. The thing we were designing for was a
person outdoors with cold hands and one free thumb, and we never saw that.
